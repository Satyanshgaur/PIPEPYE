# Case D: Power System Unit Commitment & Economic Dispatch (MILP)

## 1. Real-World Problem & Industrial Context
Unit commitment (UC) and economic dispatch (ED) constitute the primary optimization engines driving wholesale electrical power markets managed by Independent System Operators (ISOs) and Regional Transmission Organizations (RTOs)—including PJM Interconnection, ERCOT, CAISO, and Grid-India (POSOCO).

Power grid operators must determine 24-hour to 168-hour day-ahead schedules for a fleet of generating assets (baseload nuclear, supercritical thermal coal, combined-cycle gas turbines, fast-ramping open-cycle peakers, and hydro storage). The operational challenge is to commit (turn on/off) and dispatch (modulate megawatts) generation units to satisfy continuously fluctuating electrical consumer load and mandated spinning reserves at minimum total operational cost, while strictly respecting generator physical operational constraints:
- Minimum electrical generation output levels ($P^{\min}_g$).
- Dynamic inter-temporal ramping rates (limits on how quickly megawatt output can increase or decrease between consecutive hours to prevent thermal stress and blade fatigue).
- Hourly system-wide spinning reserves to safeguard against catastrophic generator trips.

## 3. Mathematical Formulation

### Sets & Indices
- $g \in \mathcal{G} = \{1, \dots, G\}$: Set of dispatchable thermal power generation units.
- $t \in \mathcal{T} = \{1, \dots, T\}$: Discrete hourly operational periods ($t = 1, \dots, 24$ or $48$).

### Decision Variables
- $u_{g, t} \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary commitment indicator of generator $g$ during hour $t$ ($1$ if online and synchronized to grid, $0$ if offline).
- $p_{g, t} \ge 0$: Active electrical power generation output dispatched from unit $g$ in hour $t$ (in Megawatts, MW).

### Parameters
- $C^{\text{fixed}}_g$: No-load fixed hourly operational and maintenance cost incurred whenever unit $g$ is committed (\$/hr).
- $C^{\text{var}}_g$: Marginal variable fuel cost per Megawatt-hour generated by unit $g$ (\$/MWh).
- $D_t$: System-wide electrical power demand in hour $t$ (MW).
- $R_t$: Required operating spinning reserve margin in hour $t$ (MW), typically $10\% - 15\%$ above demand.
- $P^{\min}_g, P^{\max}_g$: Minimum stable generation limit and maximum continuous capacity rating of unit $g$ (MW).
- $\text{RU}_g, \text{RD}_g$: Maximum allowable ramp-up and ramp-down rates of unit $g$ between adjacent hours (MW/hr).

### Objective Function
Minimize the sum of no-load commitment costs and variable generation costs across the fleet:
$$\min \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{g=1}^G \left( C^{\text{fixed}}_g u_{g, t} + C^{\text{var}}_g p_{g, t} \right)$$

### Constraints
1. **System Power Demand Balance**:
   $$\sum_{g=1}^G p_{g, t} \ge D_t \quad \forall t \in \mathcal{T}$$

2. **System Spinning Reserve Requirement**:
   $$\sum_{g=1}^G P^{\max}_g u_{g, t} \ge D_t + R_t \quad \forall t \in \mathcal{T}$$

3. **Generator Physical Operating Limits**:
   $$p_{g, t} \ge P^{\min}_g u_{g, t} \quad \forall g \in \mathcal{G}, t \in \mathcal{T}$$
   $$p_{g, t} \le P^{\max}_g u_{g, t} \quad \forall g \in \mathcal{G}, t \in \mathcal{T}$$

4. **Dynamic Inter-Temporal Ramping Envelope**:
   For each generator $g$ and consecutive hours $t \in \{2, \dots, T\}$:
   $$p_{g, t} - p_{g, t-1} \le \text{RU}_g u_{g, t-1} + P^{\min}_g (u_{g, t} - u_{g, t-1})$$
   $$p_{g, t-1} - p_{g, t} \le \text{RD}_g u_{g, t} + P^{\min}_g (u_{g, t-1} - u_{g, t})$$

## 4. Assumptions & Simplifications in PipePye
1. **Single-Bus Copper-Plate Model**: Line reactances and transmission bus angle constraints (DC power flow / line congestion) are omitted to focus specifically on the combinatorial generator commitment dynamics.
2. **Piecewise-Linear / Constant Marginal Heat Rates**: Fuel cost curves are represented with constant marginal heat rate $C^{\text{var}}_g$, capturing the standard MILP formulation without quadratic convex non-linearities.
3. **Minimum Up/Down Time Approximation**: Minimum run-time constraints are represented through ramp envelopes and commitment step transitions.

## 5. Mathematical Structure & Pre-Registered Predictions

### Structural Classification
- **Exactly 50% Binary Variables**: Every generator at every hour has exactly one binary commitment variable $u_{g, t}$ and one continuous dispatch variable $p_{g, t}$ ($n_{\text{bin}} / n = 0.50$). For the Large instance (40 units $\times$ 48 hours), the binary search space spans $2^{960} \approx 10^{289}$ states.
- **Dense Demand & Reserve Rows**: The hourly power balance and spinning reserve rows couple all generators at hour $t$ across the grid.
- **Staircase Temporal Propagation**: Dynamic ramp rate constraints link each generator between $t-1$ and $t$, creating high staircase structure ($\text{Staircase Score} > 0.993$).

### Pre-Registered Predictions
- **Algorithm Prediction**: **Branch-and-Bound with Dual Simplex Basis Warm-Starting**. Because 50% of the columns are binary, continuous solvers are completely incapable of producing operational schedules. Tree exploration is mandatory.
- **Warm-Start Dual Simplex Dominance**: Branching on generator status $u_{g, t} \in \{0, 1\}$ adds a simple bound restriction. Because bound restrictings maintain dual feasibility, warm-started Dual Simplex solves each child node in only 6 to 23 pivots, compared to 55 to 980 pivots when cold-started from scratch.
- **Hardware Prediction**: **CPU Execution**. Branch-and-bound tree maintenance and basis updates are latency-sensitive and irregular, heavily favoring CPU out-of-order execution and cache locality over GPU SIMT architectures.
